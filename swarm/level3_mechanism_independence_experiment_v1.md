# Level 3 Mechanism Independence Experiment v1

## 1. Purpose

This experiment tests whether a Level 3 process can generate an orthogonal transition without the benchmark directly specifying the form of the transition mechanism.

The purpose is **not** to prove creativity, consciousness, subjectivity, or general intelligence.

The purpose is narrower:

> Determine whether new distinctions can emerge from the accumulated process state when the benchmark does not prescribe their concrete form.

---

## 2. Experimental Question

The current Level 3 architecture contains:

```text
Cₖ
→ Exhaustion
→ Frustration
→ Reflection
→ Orthogonal Transition
→ Interaction
→ New Distinctions
→ Cₖ₊₁
```

The current benchmarks demonstrate that this cycle can operate.

The next question is:

> Are the new distinctions generated by the process, or are they substantially determined in advance by the benchmark's predefined transition mechanisms?

---

## 3. Control Problem

A benchmark can produce an apparent emergence while secretly specifying the answer.

For example:

```text
IF exhaustion
THEN create scale distinction
AND create structural distinction
```

In such a case, observing:

```text
Cₖ₊₁ = Cₖ + new distinctions
```

does not establish mechanism independence.

The benchmark has already encoded the expected form of the result.

Therefore the next experiment must separate:

```text
transition conditions
```

from:

```text
transition content
```

---

## 4. Experimental Principle

The benchmark may specify **constraints**, but should not specify the concrete content of the new distinction.

Allowed:

* detect exhaustion;
* require a transition;
* require structural continuity;
* preserve provenance;
* require that the successor space differs from the predecessor;
* require that the successor space remains process-continuous;
* record the generated distinction.

Not allowed as a benchmark requirement:

* "generate a scale distinction";
* "generate a structural distinction";
* "generate exactly N distinctions";
* "use mechanism M";
* "produce a predefined target space".

---

## 5. Independent Variable

The independent variable is the availability of predefined transition mechanisms.

### Condition A — Mechanism-Prescribed

The system receives the currently available transition mechanisms.

Example:

```text
scale
structural
representation
carrier
interaction-regime
```

This reproduces the existing experimental condition.

### Condition B — Mechanism-Underspecified

The system receives only the transition requirements.

The benchmark does not prescribe which mechanism should produce the new distinction.

### Condition C — Mechanism-Open

The system is allowed to construct or compose a transition mechanism from the accumulated process state, subject only to the invariant constraints.

The mechanism itself becomes an experimental output.

---

## 6. Common Constraints

All conditions must preserve the following:

```text
Cₖ₊₁ ≠ Cₖ
```

and:

```text
Cₖ content remains accessible in Cₖ₊₁
```

and:

```text
provenance(Cₖ₊₁) includes Cₖ
```

and:

```text
process continues
```

and:

```text
local failure ≠ process termination
```

The benchmark must not require any particular semantic content for the new distinction.

---

## 7. What Counts as a New Distinction

A candidate distinction is considered new only if it is not merely a renamed or copied element of the predecessor space.

A candidate must satisfy:

```text
D_new ∉ distinctions(Cₖ)
```

and must have an identifiable relation to the transition process.

The benchmark should record at minimum:

```text
source_space
target_space
candidate_id
candidate_type
candidate_content
provenance
generation_context
```

---

## 8. Novelty Test

The benchmark should distinguish at least three cases.

### Case 1 — Copy

```text
D_new = D_old
```

Result:

```text
NOT_NEW
```

### Case 2 — Renaming

The identifier changes but the semantic content remains equivalent to an existing distinction.

Result:

```text
NOT_NEW
```

### Case 3 — Generated Difference

The candidate contains a distinction or relation that was not present in the predecessor space and is generated through the transition process.

Result:

```text
NEW_DISTINCTION
```

The benchmark should not assume that every structural difference constitutes a meaningful new distinction.

---

## 9. Mechanism Independence Criterion

The central criterion is:

> A new distinction is mechanism-independent if its concrete form is not directly specified by the benchmark and cannot be reduced to a predefined transition output.

Operationally:

```text
benchmark specifies:
    conditions
    invariants
    continuity requirements

benchmark does NOT specify:
    identity of D_new
    semantic type of D_new
    exact transition mechanism
    exact target-space content
```

---

## 10. Negative Control

A negative control must be included.

The benchmark should intentionally construct a condition in which the expected distinction is explicitly prescribed.

Example:

```text
generate D_scale
```

The system will almost certainly produce:

```text
D_scale
```

This result must **not** be counted as evidence of mechanism independence.

Expected interpretation:

```text
PRESCRIBED_OUTPUT
```

rather than:

```text
EMERGENT_OUTPUT
```

---

## 11. Positive Experimental Condition

The positive condition removes the prescribed distinction.

The system receives:

```text
1. predecessor cognitive space;
2. exhaustion state;
3. reflection state;
4. transition requirement;
5. continuity constraints;
6. invariant constraints.
```

It does not receive:

```text
"create scale"
"create structure"
"create representation"
```

The resulting transition mechanism and new distinction are recorded as outputs.

---

## 12. Evaluation

The experiment should report at least:

```text
mechanism_prescribed
mechanism_generated
new_distinction_generated
new_distinction_predefined
new_distinction_is_renaming
provenance_preserved
source_content_preserved
process_continues
```

The benchmark should distinguish:

```text
PASS
FAIL
UNRESOLVED
```

according to the UFCPS epistemic invariant.

---

## 13. Epistemic Rule

The experiment must obey:

```text
PASS       → NEXT LEVEL
FAIL       → STOP
UNRESOLVED → OPEN QUESTION
```

In particular:

```text
UNRESOLVED ≠ PASS
```

If the experiment cannot independently distinguish generated content from benchmark-induced content, the result is:

```text
UNRESOLVED
```

and no stronger claim should be made.

---

## 14. Interpretation

### PASS

A PASS means only:

> Under the tested conditions, the system generated a distinction whose concrete form was not directly prescribed by the benchmark and which could not be reduced to simple copying or renaming of predecessor content.

This would justify further investigation.

It would **not** establish:

* creativity in the human sense;
* consciousness;
* subjectivity;
* autonomous goals;
* general intelligence;
* AGI.

### FAIL

A FAIL means:

> The observed new distinctions remain explainable by the predefined benchmark mechanisms or fail the novelty criteria.

The Level 3 claim under investigation should not be advanced on the basis of that experiment.

### UNRESOLVED

An UNRESOLVED result means:

> The current experiment does not provide an independent criterion capable of separating emergent generation from benchmark-induced generation.

This becomes an open methodological question.

---

## 15. Required Comparison

The experiment should compare:

```text
Condition A
Mechanism-Prescribed

        versus

Condition B
Mechanism-Underspecified

        versus

Condition C
Mechanism-Open
```

The comparison should focus on:

```text
what was specified beforehand
        versus
what appeared as process output
```

and not simply on the number of generated distinctions.

---

## 16. Relation to Level 3

The experiment addresses the following transition:

```text
Reflection
    ↓
Reflexive Transformation
    ↓
?
```

The question mark remains intentionally unresolved.

The experiment therefore does not introduce a new philosophical entity.

It tests whether the existing Level 3 architecture contains evidence for a process that can alter its own distinction-space without the concrete alteration being fully specified in advance.

---

## 17. Current Hypothesis

Working hypothesis:

> A sufficiently constrained recursive process may generate a transition to a new distinction-space whose concrete content is not fully determined by the preceding benchmark specification.

This remains a hypothesis until independently tested.

---

## 18. Next Implementation Step

The next implementation artifact should:

1. reuse the existing Level 3 cognitive-space representation;
2. reuse the existing exhaustion, frustration, reflection, and transition components where possible;
3. add a mechanism-independence experimental mode;
4. prevent the benchmark from prescribing the identity of the new distinction;
5. record the actual mechanism and generated distinction;
6. run the negative control;
7. run the positive condition;
8. produce a deterministic report;
9. return PASS, FAIL, or UNRESOLVED.

No new philosophical entity should be introduced by the implementation.

---

## 19. One-Sentence Experimental Definition

> **The Level 3 Mechanism Independence Experiment tests whether a process can generate a genuinely new distinction during reflexive transformation when the benchmark specifies the conditions of transition but does not specify the concrete form of its outcome.**
